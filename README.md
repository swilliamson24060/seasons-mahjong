# Seasons Mah Jong

Mah Jong solitaire through the year, with boards for its holidays and seasons. It began as a fork of Boneyard Mah Jong, the Halloween game, which goes on as a game of its own; Boneyard's Long Night is Seasons' Halloween too, and the other holidays' boards come next. [PLAN.md](PLAN.md) has the design and what is still to do.

## Run it locally

ES modules need a web server (opening the files from the file system will not work):

```bash
python3 tools/serve.py
```

Then open http://localhost:8790 to play. `tools/serve.py` serves this folder with caching turned off, so a reload always picks up edited files. It takes another port too, to run beside Boneyard (`python3 tools/serve.py 8791`), and `python3 -m http.server` works as well.

- The menu (☾) lists all twelve, to choose one: those open now first, then the rest, greyed out, each saying when it opens, or soon for one whose boards are still to come, and last the turtle, always there. On a first visit the menu comes up over a turtle.
- Choosing a holiday goes back to its board in progress, if it has one. Otherwise its boards come up, to choose one: each board is reached by clearing the one before, and those not yet reached are greyed out. The menu also goes back to the board on the table, or gives it up, which brings its holiday's boards up again.
- The game opens on the board left on the table, while its holiday is open.
- Each holiday has its own look: its colour, its sky, and its lettering, with Halloween keeping Boneyard's. While the Long Night is on the table the page wears Halloween's, and the turtle wears the holiday chosen last, tiles and all. The menu wears Seasons' own gold.
- Halloween's, Thanksgiving's, Christmas's, New Year's and Groundhog Day's boards are made: the Long Night, Thanksgiving week, the Twelve Days of Christmas, New Year's countdown and New Year's Day, and Groundhog Day's board of the day. The other holidays stay greyed out in the menu until their boards come.
- Each holiday opens weeks before its day, to build up to it, and stays open to the end of the year; Groundhog Day alone is open all year, every year. With the new year every holiday closes again, until its opening day comes round. Until a holiday opens, the menu says when it will, and its boards can't be played, from the menu or by their address. The turtle is always there. Progress and best scores are kept through the new year.
- `js/themes.js` holds the twelve: their icons and colours, when each falls, worked out for any year, as Thanksgiving, Labor Day and the beach season move, and when each opens.

## Playing

- Halloween's Long Night is thirteen boards, one an hour from 6 pm to 6 am. Each hour says what is new before it starts, and every hour reached can be played again from the Long Night's card. The board in play is kept as you go, so closing the page loses nothing.
- Each holiday keeps its own board in play, and the turtle its own, so going from one to another through the menu never loses any. Anything that would throw away a board with moves on it asks first.
- The turtle fits the screen. On a phone held upright, where the classic turtle, fifteen tiles across, would be squeezed small, the turtle swims up the screen instead: the full set of 144 again, nine across and ten down on six layers, its head at the top, a flipper at each corner and its tail below: on a Galaxy S25+ its tiles are 39px wide, where the classic's are 23. Whichever of the two gives the bigger tiles is dealt, so a computer, a tablet on its side or a phone on its side deals the classic. A turtle in play stays as it was dealt.
- The board fills the space it has, with only a sliver of room round it, so that on a phone held upright a board nine tiles across fills its width: its tiles 40px wide on a Galaxy S25+.
- Take two free tiles that match. A tile is free when nothing lies on it and its left or right side is open. The blocked tiles are dimmed, so that only the free ones need looking over (the switch for it is under the holidays in the menu), and each tile's number or letter is printed big in its top right corner, as on a playing card, with the drawing below it, to read at a glance without counting eyes or bones. The board lifts each layer up and to the right, so a taller stack beside a tile can lie over its left side or its foot, but never its top right corner: there the number always shows. The badges the twists put on tiles, a coin, a crown, a price tag, sit at the top left, clear of it, and the counts, a sparkler's, the milk's and the ice's, just under it.
- Hold a tile to see it up close, above your finger, and slide to look at the tiles around it. On a phone this is on from the start; the switch under the holidays in the menu turns it on or off on any screen.
- Pause (P) stops the clock and hides the board until you come back, and the game pauses by itself when you leave it for another app or tab. Whenever the clock is stopped on a board not yet cleared, behind the menu or an hour's first card as well, the board is hidden, so that no one can study it for nothing.
- Undo (U), Hint (H), Shuffle (S), Pause (P), sound on and off (M). Arrow keys move between free tiles, and Enter takes one. Every button has a word as well as its icon: beside it on a wide screen, under it on a phone.
- Each pair is worth 10 points and a cleared board 250, with 2 more for every second under par. A shuffle costs 100 and a hint 25; treats and undo are free. Turning over at midnight without a lost soul earns 150, and each lost soul costs 10. `js/score.js` holds the numbers.
- From midnight the tiles turn over partway through each board. Hold the moon button or Space to see the night side. A tile held up close shows its night side beside it, or with the magnifier off, turns over while you hold it. Pairs that match on both sides are the ones to take before midnight.
- Pairs of jack-o'-lanterns leave treats, whose buttons say what they do: a candle is a free hint, a skeleton key unlocks one blocked tile (press Unlock, then the tile), and a black cat is a free shuffle. A treat you have none of says what it does when you tap it.
- The turtle, and any board played by address outside a holiday's own, speaks the words of the holiday whose tiles it wears, and changes them with its tiles when another holiday is chosen. Its treats are that holiday's own, left by the tile that stands where Halloween's jack-o'-lantern does:
  - Thanksgiving's turkeys leave friends' gifts: a tip, a saved seat and musical chairs.
  - Christmas's stockings hold a star, Santa's key and a sleigh ride.
  - New Year's party hats bring party favours: a sparkler, a party popper and a rattle.
  - Groundhog Day's suns change the weather: sunshine, a warm spell and a gust of wind.
  - Valentine's boxes of chocolates hide sweet surprises: a matchmaker, the key to your heart and speed dating.

  A board that turns over in another holiday's tiles is told in that holiday's words. It's sundown and worries in Thanksgiving's, Twelfth Night and coal in Christmas's, midnight and sleepyheads in New Year's, and the question and broken hearts in Valentine's. In Groundhog Day's, it's the forecast, when the groundhog comes out to look for his shadow, and a tile left without a partner is a shadow.
- Thanksgiving week is eight boards, from the Sunday before Thanksgiving to the Sunday after. Thanksgiving's card in the menu lists them in order: each day you reach can be played again, and clearing a board opens the next. The week's board in play is kept, as the night's is, so going to the night or the turtle loses nothing. Each day's board also plays freely by its address: `#play/highway`, `#play/turkey`, `#play/cart`, `#play/potluck`, `#play/feast`, `#play/sale`, `#play/football` and `#play/alarm`.
  - On the first Sunday's, the jammed highway, there is no twist: the gentle start, its card telling the free-tile rule as traffic.
  - On Monday's, the frozen turkey, a frozen tile can't be taken until enough matches thaw it: its face shows through the ice, with the matches still to make under its number, and the turkey thaws from the outside in.
  - On Tuesday's, the shopping cart, the list has four things to find, shown in the top bar, with a price tag on each tile of them. A pair of each, taken before checkout halfway through the board, crosses it off: 25 points each, and 100 more for the whole list.
  - On Wednesday's, Friendsgiving's potluck table, each pair of a dish (a turkey, green beans or mashed potatoes) leaves a gift: a friend's tip (a free hint), a saved seat (frees a blocked tile) or musical chairs (a free shuffle).
  - On Thursday's, the feast table, the fridge in the bottom bar has three containers for leftovers. A free tile and then a container saves the tile for later: off the table, it holds nothing down, and it still matches its partner whenever that's free. Everything in the fridge has to be eaten by the end. Halfway through, everyone naps, and the clock stops for the next 10 matches.
  - On Friday's, Black Friday's sale tag, four pairs are doorbusters, with a red % tag: a pair taken with one in it is yours, for 50 points. After every 5 of your matches, another shopper grabs the first doorbuster that's free. The top bar's Deals row keeps count.
  - On Saturday's, rivalry football's football, the field runs from the end zone at the top to the one at the bottom, their tiles carrying blue and red pennants. A pair taken with a tile in each end zone is a touchdown, for 60 points.
  - On the second Sunday's, the Sunday Scaries' alarm clock, the sun goes down halfway through and every tile turns over to its Monday side, as the Long Night's do at midnight: hold the moon to see Monday coming, and take pairs that match on both sides. A tile left without a partner wakes as a worry, and worries match each other.
- The week's tiles are Thanksgiving's own, matching just as Halloween's do: cranberries, corn and leaves for the three suits, the harvest's apple, gourd, acorn and pinecone, the turkey, green beans and mashed potatoes, autumn flowers and pies, any two of which match as any two flowers or moons do, and worries for lost souls. They are the week's wherever its boards are played, and the Long Night keeps Halloween's. The turtle, and any other board played freely, wears the tiles of the holiday chosen: Thanksgiving's for Thanksgiving, Christmas's for Christmas, and Halloween's for the rest until their own are made. Choosing another holiday with the turtle on the table changes its tiles with its colours.
- The week has its own music, as the Long Night has Bach's Toccata on a pipe organ: a banjo in a farmhouse parlour plays "Over the River and Through the Wood" a note for each match, and its last line, over a guitar's oom-pah, for a cleared board; when the week is seen through, a parlour reed organ plays "We Gather Together". A shuffle rakes autumn leaves, a hint rings a handbell, a treat the dinner triangle, and with no pairs left, a turkey gobbles. The turtle plays it too while Thanksgiving is the holiday chosen.
- Christmas is the Twelve Days: twelve boards, one for each gift of the song, from a partridge in a pear tree to twelve drummers drumming, opening on December 1st. Christmas's card in the menu lists them in order, as Thanksgiving's does the week: each day you reach can be played again, clearing a board opens the next, and the bests add up to a Christmas score. The twelve days' board in play is kept beside the week's and the night's. Each is shaped by its gift, and they grow through the twelve days, as the song does, from 80 tiles to 128; each also plays freely by its address while Christmas is open. Its tiles are Christmas's own, matching just as Halloween's do: ornaments, candy canes and presents for the three suits, the song's four birds, the partridge, the turtle dove, the French hen and the calling bird, the stocking, the tree and the snowman, Christmas's plants, poinsettia, holly, mistletoe and the Christmas rose, and snowflakes, any two of which match as any two flowers or moons do, though no two are alike, and lumps of coal for lost souls, glowing like embers.
  - On the first day's, a partridge perched at the top of a pear tree (`#play/peartree`), there is no twist: the gentle start, its card telling the free-tile rule by the pears, which hang free.
  - On the second day's, two turtle doves beak to beak, making a heart (`#play/doves`), every kind of tile comes as two pairs: take a kind's second pair straight after its first, and they're lovebirds, for 30 points. The top bar counts them (🕊️).
  - On the third day's, a French hen sitting on her nest (`#play/hen`), a dozen tiles are still eggs: there's no telling what's inside one until it comes free and hatches. The top bar counts the eggs still to hatch (🥚), and a tile held up close shows only its egg.
  - On the fourth day's, a songbird singing on a branch (`#play/songbird`), birds of a feather flock together: take a pair whose two tiles touch, side by side or one just above the other, for 20 points. The top bar counts them (🐦).
  - On the fifth day's, five rings linked as the five spots on a die are (`#play/rings`), take every tile of a ring, and it turns to gold where it lay, for 50 points: a cleared board shows all five gold rings. It is dealt a ring at a time, so that a ring's tiles mostly match each other, and a ring can be cleared long before the board is. The top bar counts the gold rings (💍).
  - On the sixth day's, a goose sitting on her nest of eggs (`#play/goose`), goslings follow their mother in a line: a pair taken beside or just under the pair taken before it follows on, and six in a line score 60 points. The free tiles a pair could follow on from carry a gosling, and the top bar counts the line so far (🐣).
  - On the seventh day's, a swan gliding on a still lake (`#play/swan`), the bottom half of the board is the top half's reflection, its tiles tinged with blue. Seven tiles match their own reflection, the tile as far below the water as they are above it: take a tile and its reflection together, a swan, for 50 points; take either with another tile, and she swims off. The top bar counts the swans found, out of those found and still to find (🦢).
  - On the eighth day's, a milk pail (`#play/pail`), eight pairs are pails of fresh milk, each tile marked 🥛. Once either of a pail's tiles is free, its milk keeps for eight matches, counted down on both: dig out the other and take the two together while it's fresh, for 40 points. Take either with another tile and it spills; leave it too long and it sours. The top bar counts the pails taken fresh (🥛).
  - On the ninth day's, a dancer mid-twirl (`#play/dancer`), every nine matches the music changes: each free tile dances off with a tile still held down, and they change places, twirling, so tiles held down come free and the pairs that were free go back under. The board is always left so it can be won. The top bar counts down to the next change (💃).
  - On the tenth day's, a crown (`#play/crown`), ten tiles are lords, gilded and crowned 👑: a lord is free wherever he is, under other tiles or hemmed in, and goes with any free tile of his kind. Take one while he's still held down, and he's leapt, for 20 points; or keep him to get out of a tight spot. The top bar counts the lords still on the board (👑).
  - On the eleventh day's, a set of bagpipes (`#play/bagpipes`), eleven tiles are pipers, tinged green and marked 🎶. Take a piper with a tile of his kind, and he plays: the rest of his kind still on the board follow him out, from wherever they are, buried or not, for 15 points each. The top bar counts the pipers still on the board (🎶).
  - On the twelfth day's, a drum with its sticks crossed above it (`#play/drum`), it's Twelfth Night: halfway through, the drummers drum and every tile turns over, as the Long Night's do at midnight. Hold the moon to see the other side coming, and before then take pairs that match on both sides: a tile left without a partner turns to coal, and coal matches coal (🪨).
- The twelve days have their own music: a music box by the fireside plays "The Twelve Days of Christmas", the fifth verse a note for each match, and for a cleared board the verse of its day, the sixth to the eleventh going from their own gift straight down to the five gold rings; when Twelfth Night is cleared, the twelfth verse whole, every gift counting down, and the church bells. Sleigh bells jingle with the bass notes, and for a shuffle; a hint rings a little silver bell, a treat is a present unwrapped, and with no pairs left, a lump of coal drops into the stocking. The turtle plays it too while Christmas is the holiday chosen.
- New Year's is the countdown: ten boards, counted down from 10 to 1, each shaped as its own number, as the giant numbers on the screens in Times Square are, and after them New Year's Day, the morning after, with a resolution to keep, opening on December 1st. Each number is drawn bold, with a ridge along it on the layer above, or on the 2 rails, and the boards grow toward midnight. New Year's card in the menu lists the eleven in order, as Christmas's does its days: each board you reach can be played again, clearing a board opens the next, and the bests add up to a New Year's score. Beside them stands the ball on its pole, dropping a notch with each board of the countdown cleared, and landing at midnight, halfway through the 1, the new year's sign beneath it lighting up. Each board also plays freely by its address while New Year's is open. Its tiles are New Year's own, matching just as Halloween's do: clocks at five to midnight, party horns and calendar pages for the three suits; four who see the new year in, Father Time, Baby New Year, a night owl in a party hat and a polar bear in a swim ring, for the plunge; a party hat, champagne and New Year's ball; lucky charms, a four-leaf clover, a horseshoe, a ladybird and a lucky pig, and the seasons of the year to come, any two of which match as any two flowers or moons do; and sleepyheads for lost souls, glowing a dreamy lilac.
  - On the 10 (`#play/ten`), the countdown begins, with no twist: the gentle start, its card telling the free-tile rule by the ridge down the 1.
  - On the 9 (`#play/nine`), the fireworks: nine tiles are rockets 🚀. Take a rocket with a tile of its kind and it bursts, lighting up every tile touching it, beside it, just above or below it, or under it, in a firework's lilac ✨. Two lit tiles taken together are a lit pair, for 30 points, so a rocket is best set off where pairs are waiting. The top bar counts the rockets still to set off (🚀).
  - On the 8 (`#play/eight`), the disco ball 🪩: its beam lies across the board a row deep and sweeps down it, a row further with every match, starting again at the top once it reaches the bottom. A pair taken with both its tiles in the beam, a tile half in it counting, dances in the spotlight, for 30 points, so pairs are set up for where the beam is heading. The top bar counts the pairs that have danced (🪩).
  - On the 7 (`#play/seven`), a lucky seven: a lucky coin 🪙 is hidden in one of its tiles, on the bottom layer, held down at the start. After every pair you take, it says how close that pair came, by the nearer of its tiles: 🔥 hot, a tile away or less, 🌡️ warm, three or less, or 🧊 cold, the sign floating up where the pair was taken and staying in the top bar. The coin turns up, gleaming, as soon as its tile comes free, so it pays to dig where it's hot: found at once it's worth 200 points, 5 less for every match before, and never less than 25.
  - On the 6 (`#play/six`), the balloon drop: as the board starts, and again every six matches, a net opens and three balloons 🎈 drop onto free tiles, each with no other tile of its kind free. Taking a balloon's tile before the net opens again pops it, for 15 points, so it's a race to dig out a tile of its kind; the balloons still left then float away. The top bar counts the balloons to pop and the matches until the net opens again (🎈 3 · ⏳ 6).
  - On the 5 (`#play/five`), sparklers: five sparklers 🎇 are planted among the tiles, grey and unlit while they're held down. Each lights as soon as its tile comes free and burns down over five matches, its badge counting them, amber at the last two: taken while it still burns, it scores 10 points for every match it has left, so a sparkler is best lit with a tile of its kind already free to take it with. Burnt out, it's a tile like any other. The top bar counts the sparklers still to take (🎇).
  - On the 4 (`#play/four`), confetti: every tile has caught a fleck of confetti in one of four colours, told apart by their shapes too: a red heart ♥, a gold star ★, a silver dot ● or a blue diamond ◆. Of a kind with four tiles on the board, two caught the same colour, a couple, and the other two neither its colour nor each other's: a couple taken together is a perfect match ✨, for 5 points, so with three or four of a kind free, which go together matters. A kind with one pair has no couple. A shuffle scatters the confetti again. The top bar counts the perfect matches (✨).
  - On the 3 (`#play/three`), shooting stars: as the board starts, and again every three matches, a shooting star 🌠 streaks across one row of the board, and the row glows a starry blue until the next. A pair taken with both its tiles in that row, a tile half in it counting, makes a wish, for 15 points: one wish to a star, and the glow fades once it's made. Each star crosses a row with two free tiles of a kind in it if there is one, and if not, one with two tiles of a kind to dig out; near the end, with nowhere a wish can be made, none crosses. The top bar counts the wishes made and the matches until the next star (🌠 0 · ⏳ 3).
  - On the 2 (`#play/two`), a toast for two: two glasses of champagne 🥂 stand at the two ends of the 2, one in its curl and one at the end of its foot. With every match, each glides one tile along the 2 toward the other, unless a tile lies in its way on the layer above, as one does on every other place along it: the tiles in the glasses' way are ringed in gold. Clear them, and when the glasses meet they clink, a toast worth 5 points for every pair still on the board, 270 at the soonest, on the fifteenth match. The top bar counts the places still between the glasses (🥂 29).
  - On the 1 (`#play/one`), midnight, on the full set of 144 tiles: halfway through, the ball lands, the clock strikes twelve, and every tile turns over to the new year's side, as the Long Night's do at midnight. Hold the moon, or Space, to see it coming, and before midnight take pairs that match on both sides. A tile left without a partner misses midnight, fast asleep, and sleepyheads (😴) match each other. Fireworks go up as the clock finishes striking. The top bar counts down to midnight (🕛 36 to midnight), with the sleepyheads there would be if the tiles turned over now.
  - On New Year's Day (`#play/january`), after midnight, a wall calendar turned to January, its rings hooked over the month's band and its five weeks of days below, and a resolution: no sweets. Six sweets sit on top of the board, free from the start, each tinted a frosting pink with its treat on it (🍩 🍪 🧁 🍫 🍬 🍰) and holding a tile down. Leave them alone through January, the first 31 matches: every match held out scores 5 points, and the whole month 100 more, 255 in all. Take one before then and the resolution is broken; choosing one warns that it would. After January, the sweets are fair game. The top bar counts the days the resolution has held (📅 12/31), then says it was kept (💪) or broken (💔).
- New Year's has its own music: a piano in a ballroom plays "Auld Lang Syne", a note for each match, and for a cleared board the chorus's last line, "we'll tak' a cup o' kindness yet", the left hand under it; when New Year's Day is cleared and the countdown seen through, the whole chorus, the party horns and the room cheering. Midnight's fanfare is the band's brass. A shuffle blows a party horn, a hint touches a champagne glass, a treat pops a cork, and with no pairs left, a party horn runs out of breath. The turtle plays it too while New Year's is the holiday chosen.
- Groundhog Day, open all year, every year, is the board of the day: a groundhog peeking out of his burrow, dealt afresh each day from the date, so that everyone has the same board that day. Play it as often as you like, the same tiles in the same places every time, until you get it just right, and come back tomorrow for a new one. Groundhog Day's card shows your best today, the days in a row you've cleared the board of the day, and the week's days with their bests. Clear it under par and the groundhog sees no shadow, an early spring; otherwise he sees it, and it's six more weeks of winter. Giving it up starts the same board over. Its tiles are Groundhog Day's own, matching just as Halloween's do: burrows, candles for Candlemas and scrolls for the three suits; four weather prophets, the groundhog in his top hat, a hedgehog, a badger and a bear, just woken; the sun, a weather vane and a cloud; spring's first flowers, a snowdrop, a crocus, a daffodil and a tulip, and songbirds, a robin, a bluebird, a cardinal and a chickadee, any two of which match as any two flowers or moons do; and his shadow for lost souls, glowing the pale blue of snow at dusk. Its music is Vivaldi's Four Seasons on a harpsichord: Spring's tune, a note for each match, and for the board cleared, as the groundhog forecasts, Spring's opening for an early spring, or Winter's, its strings shivering, for six more weeks of it. A shuffle is the groundhog digging, a hint a cardinal's whistle, a treat a candle lit for Candlemas, and with no pairs left, he sees his shadow and scurries back down his burrow.
- Valentine's Day is a love story: eight boards, from Cupid taking aim to be mine, each a moment of it, as `PLAN.md` plans them, reached in turn from its card in the menu, as Christmas's twelve days are, with the Valentine's score, the bests added up. The first is Cupid's bow drawn, an arrow on its string pointing up, ready to fly (`#play/bow`), the gentle start, with no twist, its card telling the free-tile rule by the bow's string, whose tiles are free from the start. The second is love at first sight, a heart pierced by Cupid's arrow (`#play/heart`), with Cupid's arrows: as the board starts, and every eight matches, his arrow strikes two tiles of a kind, and they're smitten, blushing pink with a heart on each counting down to his next arrow. Take the two together before it flies for love at first sight, 30 points; take either with another tile and a heart is broken; leave them and they get over it. Each couple's hearts show, it has to be dug for, and the hints' way takes it in time, so it can always be had. The top bar counts the couples (💘 2). The third is a secret admirer, a valentine in its envelope (`#play/envelope`), its flap closed in a V and sealed with a heart, with sweethearts: eight tiles carry candy hearts, each with half a sweet nothing, BE and MINE, TRUE and LOVE, HUG and ME, SWEET and TALK, a message's two halves the same pastel. Take the pair with one half, and then, as the very next match, the pair with the other, and the message is said, 40 points; take anything between and it goes unsaid. The half still to take beats until the next match, a tile held up close shows its word in full, and the top bar counts the messages said (💌 2/4). The deal puts each message on two pairs its own order takes one after the other, so following the hints says all four. The fourth is a dozen roses, a vase of roses (`#play/roses`), its twelve roses raised over the bouquet, in bloom: they start in bud, and from the third match one opens every three matches, from the top down, blooming for six, a rose on it counting down. Take a rose in bloom with any tile of its kind and it joins the bouquet, 20 points; a bud taken is wasted, and a rose left too long wilts. The deal takes every rose in bloom in its own order, so following the hints gathers all twelve, and the top bar counts the bouquet (🌹 5/12). The fifth is the first date, dinner by candlelight (`#play/candles`), two tall candles on the table: each candle's glow tinges the tiles round its flame gold, his on the left and hers on the right, and a pair with a tile in each glow is candlelit, 20 points. Every six matches the candles burn down and their glow sinks a row toward the table, until they gutter out. The deal's own order takes six candlelit pairs or more, the hints take one whenever they can, and the top bar counts them, with the matches until the glow sinks (🕯️ 3 · ⏳ 4). The sixth is a box of chocolates (`#play/chocolates`), nine in three rows inside the box's walls, each a top, the chocolate under it and its paper cup: bite into one by taking its top, and eat the rest of it, cup and all, before biting into another, and it's savoured, 30 points; bite into another first and the one left is just eaten. The chocolate bitten into is ringed in brown, the walls give the chocolates' tiles partners, the deal eats them one at a time so following the hints savours all nine, and the top bar counts them (🍫 3/9). The seventh is hugs and kisses (`#play/xo`), an X over an O: a pair from the X is a kiss and from the O a hug, and kisses and hugs taken in turn, four in a row, make an XOXO, 15 points; two of a kind together, or a pair across the letters, start it again. It's dealt a letter at a time, so following the hints makes fifteen XOXOs, the letter the run wants next blushes, and the top bar shows the run and counts them (💋 3 · XOX). The eighth is be mine, a diamond ring (`#play/ring`), the full set of 144: halfway through, the question is popped 💍 and every tile turns over, the clock counting down to it (💍 3 to the question). Take pairs that match on both sides before then; a tile left without a partner is a broken heart, and broken hearts match each other. The score says the answer is yes, with no broken hearts, or counts them, and clearing it plays the theme's whole second time. Its tiles are Valentine's own, matching just as Halloween's do: hearts, Cupid's arrows and valentines sealed in their envelopes for the three suits; four valentines' puns, a bumblebee with hearts for wings, a barn owl whose face is a heart, a teddy bear holding a heart and a kitten whose nose is one; a box of chocolates, a love potion and a paper lace valentine; love's flowers, a red rose, violets, a pink carnation and a daisy, and candy hearts saying BE MINE, XOXO, LOVE YOU and HUG ME, any two of which match as any two flowers or moons do; and a broken heart for lost souls, glowing a tender pink. Its music is Liszt's Liebestraum on a harp: the theme, a note for each match over its bass, and for a board cleared its last line, the harmony rippling under it, or when the question is answered, its whole second time and the harp swept up. A shuffle is love letters shuffled, a hint a kiss blown, a treat a box of chocolates opened, and with no pairs left, a heart breaks.
- April Showers is a season, from showers to flowers: eight boards, from the first shower to May Day, as `PLAN.md` plans them, made one at a time. The first is the first shower, a rain cloud (`#play/cloud`), its biggest puff billowing up in the middle and its raindrops falling beneath it in three slanting lines, the gentle start, with no twist, its card telling the free-tile rule by the raindrops, every one free from the start. The second is umbrella weather, an open umbrella (`#play/umbrella`), its canopy a dome peaked at the top, its rim scalloped in five points and its handle hooked at the foot, with raindrops: as the board starts and every four matches, a raindrop 💧 lands on the umbrella's peak and runs down the canopy along one of its five ribs, a row with every match, dripping off at the rim, the tiles still in its way ringed in blue. Take the tile a drop is on, with any tile of its kind, to catch it, 20 points; over a place whose tiles are all taken, it runs on over nothing. Eleven drops land, the last with six matches left to run its rib. The deal's own order catches all but the odd one, so following the hints does, and the top bar counts the drops caught and the matches until the next lands (💧 3 · ⏳ 2). A raindrop lands with a pat and a plip, is caught with a bright plip, and drips off with a plop. While the rest are made they play by their addresses, wearing Halloween's tiles until April Showers' own are drawn, and April Showers says soon in the menu.
- Any board can also be played freely by address: `#play/turtle` for the classic turtle, `#play/upright_turtle` for the upright one, or `#play/raven/12345/40` for the raven, dealt from seed 12345, turning over after 40 matches. A new deal (N) deals it again. The board played freely is kept like the night's, so a reload goes on with it, and a Thanksgiving or Christmas day's board, or a board of New Year's countdown, plays by its own rules. A holiday's board plays by address only while the holiday is open; before then, its address deals a turtle. A holiday whose boards are still being made plays them by address, to try them.

## The leaderboard

The leaderboard, on the Long Night's card, lists the best scores at each hour, and for the whole night: each player's bests added up. The first time a player clears a board, its card offers to put them on it under a name of their choosing, and from then on their bests go up as they make them. Their name can be changed, or taken off with their scores, from the leaderboard's card. Free play doesn't count. Seasons has a Firebase project of its own, `seasons-mahjong`, as Boneyard's keeps Boneyard's scores; `js/firebase-config.js` names it, and without a project there, the game shows no leaderboard.

Players sign in anonymously, which gives each browser an id of its own and no account to make, and the scores are kept in Firestore, at `boards/{board}/scores/{player}`, one entry a player on each board, holding their best. The game speaks to both through their REST APIs (`js/firebase.js`), so it needs no Firebase library.

[firestore.rules](firestore.rules) says what players may do: anyone may read the boards, and each player may write only their own entries, which can only get better. A score must also be one its board could give at the fastest a hand could clear it, a pair a second, so the rules hold each board's tiles and par; test.html checks them against the game's own, and they need publishing again if the boards change. So far they know only the Long Night's hours: the other holidays' boards will need adding, under names of their own, as they come.

Setting up the project, in the [Firebase console](https://console.firebase.google.com), takes five steps. The console's menus move about, but within a project its address is `console.firebase.google.com/project/<project id>/...`, and the pages below can be reached by changing the end of it:

1. Create a project. Google Analytics isn't needed, and the free plan is plenty.
2. Authentication (`authentication/providers`): Get started, then under Sign-in method turn on Anonymous. Leave its automatic clean-up off: a player whose anonymous account had been deleted could no longer change their own entries.
3. Firestore Database (`firestore`): Create database, keeping the id `(default)` and the Standard edition, in a location near your players, in production mode.
4. In Firestore Database, under Rules: replace what's there with the whole of `firestore.rules`, and Publish.
5. Project settings (`settings/general`), Your apps: add a Web app (no Firebase Hosting; npm or a script tag, it doesn't matter, as the game uses neither) and copy its `apiKey` and `projectId` into `js/firebase-config.js`. The key begins `AIza`; the `appId` beside it, `1:` and a long number, is something else. Neither is a secret: every web app sends them from the browser, and the rules decide what anyone can do.

## Online

The game lives at https://swilliamson24060.github.io/seasons-mahjong/, served by GitHub Pages from the `main` branch of this repository, as it is: `.nojekyll` keeps GitHub from processing it. Pushing `main` puts a new version online within a minute or two, and players get it the next time they load the game with a network.

Boneyard lives beside it, as a game of its own, at https://swilliamson24060.github.io/boneyard-mahjong/. The two share a site, swilliamson24060.github.io, and with it a browser's storage, so Seasons keeps its saves under names that begin `seasons.` and its offline copy in caches named `seasons-`, and neither game touches the other's.

## On itch.io

`python3 tools/itch.py` bundles the game for itch.io as `dist/seasons-mahjong.zip`: `index.html` at its root, every file the game loads (the list in `sw.js`, with `sw.js` itself) and nothing else. The zip is built, not kept in git, so build it again after a change. On itch.io:

1. Create a new project, with Kind of project set to HTML.
2. Under Uploads, upload the zip, and tick This file will be played in the browser.
3. Under Embed options, a viewport of 960 by 640 suits it, though the game fits whatever size it is given; tick Mobile friendly, and Fullscreen button.
4. Save, and view the page.

`python3 tools/shots.py` makes the page's pictures in `dist/itch/`, from the game as it is, in a few seconds: the cover, 630 by 500 as itch.io asks, and five screenshots, three from a computer and two from a phone. `tools/cover.html` draws the cover from the game's own tiles and lettering, and `tools/stage.html` sets up each screenshot's scene with moves from the game's own winning line, so they come out the same each time, and anew after a change. It needs Google Chrome, which draws the pictures headless (or the `CHROME` variable saying where a Chrome is), and macOS's `sips`, to cut out the phone pictures. For now the pictures are all Halloween's.

There the game plays in a frame in itch.io's page, so it leaves out its service worker: playing offline is for the game at its own address.

## Offline, and on a phone's home screen

Once loaded, the game plays offline: `sw.js`, a service worker, keeps every file it needs, and the fonts. While there is a network it still asks the server for each file, rather than trusting the browser's ten-minute copy, so a new version shows on the next load. A file added to the game must also be added to `FILES` in `sw.js`, with a new name for `CACHE`; the test page checks that nothing is missing. `manifest.webmanifest` lets a phone install it from the browser (Share, then Add to Home Screen, on an iPhone), with its own icon, for now still Boneyard's jack-o'-lantern. `tools/icons.py` draws the icon in plain Python and writes `icons/`: `python3 tools/icons.py`.

## Tests and sheets
